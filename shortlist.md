# Shortlist — "One Word From Grandma"

The gate is trivial (`/output/entry.txt`, 1–30 chars, one token). The whole bounty is the pick.
So this is not a list of words I like; it is the argument for one word over thirty-six.

The judge is a text-message critic who has read a thousand grandma words. That means the bar is
not "funny" in the abstract — it is *"I have read this exact joke before"* avoidance, while still
being readable at a glance with no caption. Two things therefore matter more than cleverness:
the token has to sound like her thumb, and it has to not be the thing everyone else thought of.

## The obvious ones (rejected, and why they lose)

| word | why it loses |
| --- | --- |
| `robot` | The entry everybody writes. Zero grandma, zero warmth, and the critic has seen it 200 times. |
| `computer` | Same problem, one rung more specific. It names the machine correctly, which is the opposite of baffled. |
| `scary` | Feelings without voice. Fits a thunderstorm, a dog, a diagnosis. Fails the "tied to AI" test. |
| `Skynet` | A film reference, not a grandmother. She has never heard of it, so it cannot be her voice. |
| `Hal` | Same failure: a name from a film she did not see. Also unreadable alone. |
| `Alexa` | Brand name, and the least interesting AI she actually owns. Also the universal guess. |
| `witchcraft` | The single most popular "old lady meets AI" word on the internet. Dead on arrival. |
| `blackmagic` | Witchcraft with extra syllables. Same forfeit. |
| `gobbledygook` | Good grandma word, wrong target — it describes the *output*, not the machine, and it is a staple guess. |
| `hocuspocus` | Same family as witchcraft. Reads as a caption for an entry, not an entry. |
| `Terminator` | Film, no warmth, no bafflement. A costume, not a grandmother. |
| `R2D2` | Punctuation-free but still a prop. No older woman texts this. |

## The near-misses (good grandma, weak tie to AI — or good AI, weak grandma)

| word | problem |
| --- | --- |
| `contraption` | Perfectly her voice, properly baffled, warm. But she calls a lawnmower a contraption too: it could fit any subject, so it names her attitude rather than the machine. Strong runner-up. |
| `doohickey` | Warmer still, and the "she can't name it" joke is real — but it is a word for *any* object. The AI disappears behind it. |
| `gizmo` | Too modern, too generic, and slightly cute in a way she would not be. |
| `whatchamacallit` | The joke is her failing to name it, which is charming and also exactly why it fails: it refuses to be about AI at all. |
| `addingmachine` | Genuinely funny deflation — she reduces a miracle to the thing she used at the office. But 13 letters mashed together reads like a typo spill rather than a word she sent. |
| `typewriter` | Her era, her machine, and text is what AI makes. No laugh though: nobody is surprised by it. |
| `mixmaster` | Lovely idea (it mixes everything it ever read into one answer) but it is a brand, and it needs the explanation to land. |
| `percolator` | Same charm, same problem: the AI tie is a metaphor I invented, not something she would say. |
| `jukebox` | A machine that answers requests. Cute, but audio-only, so the tie to a chatbot is decorative. |
| `ouija` | Intellectually the best analogue — you ask, it answers, nobody knows how — but it is spooky, not warm, and criterion two is weighted second. |
| `golem` | The most *accurate* word on this page: a clay thing animated by words, obedient, literal, dangerous when misworded. It is also folklore, not something an 80-year-old texts. |
| `automaton` | Victorian and precise, and precisely why it sounds like a museum label instead of a thumb. |
| `newfangled` | Real grandma adjective for machines, but it is an attitude, not an observation, and it has no joke in it. |
| `tarnation` | Perfect grandma exclamation, zero machine content. Could caption a hailstorm. |
| `malarkey` | Same: her voice, nothing to do with a machine. |
| `blessitsheart` | The warmest thing on this page and the funniest reading (she pities the machine). Fails criterion four outright — she says it about puppies, nephews and bad weather. |
| `motormouth` | My strongest runner-up: 1950s slang for a talker, applied to something that will not stop producing paragraphs, with "motor" quietly doing the machine work. Lost only because the judge can read it as a word about a person. |
| `chatterbox` | Warmer than motormouth and "chat" glues it to chatbots — but it is a common guess, and it reads slightly like a nickname for a child. |
| `knowitall` | Very good: answers everything, never admits doubt, exactly the precocious grandchild she already has one of. Loses narrowly — it accuses a *personality*, and the machine is only implied. |
| `copycat` | She scolds grandkids with it, and AI does copy everything. But it fits a sibling as easily as a server. |
| `tattletale` | Funny and hers; the AI does report on you. Too narrow, and a little cold for the warmth criterion. |
| `busybody` | Exasperated grandmother, meddling machine. Reads as a neighbour, not a computer. |
| `compooter` | The phonetic misspelling. Funnier to look at than `computor`, but it reads as a five-year-old typing, not a seventy-eight-year-old. Wrong generation of error. |
| `artifical` | A real grandma typo of a real AI word, and it has no joke: alone, it is just a missing vowel. |
| `kidsthesedays` | Two words pretending to be one, and it is about kids, not machines. |

## The pick

**`computor`** (8 characters, one token, lower-case, no punctuation)

Three things decided it.

1. **It is the token her thumb would actually produce.** Not a word *about* her voice — her voice
   itself, misspelled the way she misspells it, lower-case, unpunctuated, sent at 6:40 a.m. with no
   follow-up. Every other candidate on this page is me impersonating a grandmother. This one is the
   artifact.
2. **The typo is the whole joke, and it needs no caption.** `computer` is a machine. `computor` is a
   machine a woman is a little afraid of and will not spell correctly because she is not fully
   convinced it is a real thing. The missing `e` is the bafflement. That is criterion two and
   criterion three in the same six keystrokes, and it survives being read alone.
3. **It takes the obvious answer and refuses it.** `computer` is the guess that loses. `computor` is
   not `computer`: one is a noun, the other is a grandmother's opinion of the noun. The banned list
   is full of words people submit because they name the machine correctly; this is the only entry on
   my shortlist that names it incorrectly on purpose, which is the freshest available move that stays
   instantly readable.

`motormouth` was the closest call and would have been the pick on a board that rewarded cleverness
over voice. This board weights "reads as the word she would text" first, then voice, then the laugh.
On that ordering, the misspelling wins: it is not a joke told about a grandmother, it is the
grandmother, and the laugh arrives before the judge has decided to give it.
